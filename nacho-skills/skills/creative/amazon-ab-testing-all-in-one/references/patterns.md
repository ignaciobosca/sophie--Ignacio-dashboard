# Proven A/B Testing Patterns — Full Catalog

This reference contains every validated pattern from real A/B tests, organized by category. Each pattern includes the source test, the metrics impact, and when to apply it.

> **IMPORTANT:** All patterns in this file are subject to the **Strict Rules (1-7)** defined in the main SKILL.md. In particular: never change packaging (Rule 1), never change colors (Rule 2), never change form factor (Rule 3), verify unit count (Rule 4), fill maximum space (Rule 5), added elements must connect to the product (Rule 6), and results must be consistent across concepts (Rule 7). If a pattern below seems to contradict a Strict Rule, the Strict Rule wins.

## 1. Image Simplification Patterns

### Pattern: Remove Visual Clutter
**Source:** Glow C Beauty Kit A/B Test
**Result:** CVR doubled after removing visual noise from the main image
**When to use:** Any image that feels "busy" at thumbnail size — multiple overlapping elements, too many benefit badges, complex backgrounds
**How to apply:**
- Remove any element that doesn't directly communicate what the product IS
- Reduce the number of benefit callouts to 2-3 max
- Increase white space between elements
- If showing a multi-pack, space items evenly with breathing room

### Pattern: One Hero Product, Not Everything
**Source:** Internal polling — cleaner versions win 20:1+ consistently
**Result:** Santino's turmeric soap: 25 votes for clean vs 1 for cluttered; Petra's images: 22 vs 2
**When to use:** When the current image tries to show every feature, every angle, every benefit at once. For SINGLE-UNIT products only.
**How to apply:**
- Pick ONE angle that best represents the product
- Remove unnecessary visual clutter (badges, callouts, extra props)
- Let the product breathe — use white space intentionally
**IMPORTANT:** This pattern does NOT mean "show fewer items than the customer is buying." If the product is a 5-pack, show all 5. If it comes in 5 colors, show all 5 colors. "Simplify" means cleaner composition and fewer unrelated elements — NOT hiding pack quantity or color variety. See "Multi-Pack & Color Variety Patterns" section.

### Pattern: Swedish Dishcloth Approach — Keyword Must Pop
**Source:** Daian's Swedish Dishcloth A/B test + Hakim's feedback
**Result:** Version where "SWEDISH DISHCLOTH" popped was preferred, but only when the product itself was still clearly visible
**When to use:** When the product name/type isn't immediately obvious from the image alone
**How to apply:**
- Make the primary keyword/product type the most visually prominent text
- Don't let decorative patterns on the product dilute the message
- Balance keyword visibility with product visibility — both matter

## 2. Premium Rendering Patterns

### Pattern: 3D Render Look with Realistic Highlights
**Source:** Macray's competitor comparison (red vs blue bottle)
**Result:** The premium-looking red bottle was outselling by 50% (same price, same ratings)
**Key elements identified by team:**
- Pump/cap positioned on the natural side (left for most products)
- Subtle but visible specular highlights on the surface
- Realistic reflections that suggest the product is physical
- Professional color grading (slightly warm tones for health/beauty)
**When to use:** Supplements, beauty products, skincare, any bottled/packaged product
**How to apply:**
- Use AI to generate a "product photography studio" look
- Add subtle rim lighting and a soft gradient reflection on the surface
- Ensure the label is crisp and readable
- Color-match the bottle/packaging to stand out on Amazon's white results page

### Pattern: Clean Shadow, Not Cheap Shadow
**Source:** Dragana's feedback on Lord Win's wallet images + multiple threads
**Result:** "Shadow is too distracting and sometimes cut in a very visible way — the edge of the shadow makes the whole image look cheap"
**When to use:** Every single main image
**How to apply:**
- Use a very soft, diffused shadow (never hard/sharp)
- Shadow should be barely noticeable — just enough to ground the product
- Check the shadow edge — if it's visible as a line, it's too harsh
- When in doubt, go with no shadow rather than a bad shadow

## 3. Product-in-Context Patterns

### Pattern: Show Product in Use
**Source:** Kelly's food containers test — containers with food beat empty ones; Nina's suggestion
**Result:** Team unanimously preferred the version with food inside
**When to use:** Any product where "in use" makes the value proposition clearer — containers, kitchen tools, organizers, pet products, moving bags
**How to apply:**
- Show the product doing what it does (container holding food, organizer with items inside, moving bag packed with folded clothes)
- Keep the context item secondary — the product is still the star
- Context elements MUST be logically connected to the product — things that go IN it, ON it, or that it's designed FOR. No random props.
- Nina's technique: Keep the main product clean/empty but add a smaller inset showing it in use
- For multi-packs: show the hero item in context, with remaining items visible behind/beside it in their different colors

### Pattern: Popular Dog Breeds for Pet Products
**Source:** Celia's dog product test + Dragana's research
**Result:** Golden Retrievers and French Bulldogs consistently perform best
**Key insight:** "If you google top breed in USA — you'll see it's Frenchie as of 2026" (Dragana); "Golden is always on top" for testing (David)
**When to use:** Any pet product where the image includes a dog
**How to apply:**
- Default to Golden Retriever or French Bulldog
- If the partner has a specific breed associated with the brand, test both their breed and a popular breed
- Dog should be clearly visible, not hidden behind the product
- Position matters: dog should complement the product, not compete with it

### Pattern: Italian Flag / Origin Indicators
**Source:** Kelly's olive oil listing update
**Result:** Added Italian flag + dropper + moved badge to corner for a cleaner, more premium look
**When to use:** Products with a strong country-of-origin story (Italian olive oil, Japanese supplements, Korean skincare)
**How to apply:**
- Small, tasteful origin indicator (flag icon, not a big banner)
- Position in a corner where it doesn't compete with the product
- Combine with premium rendering for maximum effect

## 4. Packaging & Box Patterns

### Pattern: Product Box + Key Benefits
**Source:** Oleg's mattress protector tests (4 rounds)
**Result:** Box alone lost to original. Box + protector lost. Box + size info slightly won. Final creative concept won by 71%.
**Critical learning:** The box alone doesn't work — it needs to be combined with the actual product AND a strong visual concept
**When to use:** Products that come in branded packaging, especially supplements, health products, home goods
**How to apply:**
- Show the box at an angle (not flat/front-facing)
- Place the actual product next to or emerging from the box
- Add 1-2 key benefit callouts (size, quantity, main selling point)
- DON'T rely on the box alone — it's not enough

### Pattern: Packaging Design Preferences
**Source:** Dragana's packaging design poll (cat product)
**Result:** Preferred: cleaner design (#1) but with the emotional element (cat photo) and trust icon (heart) from #2
**When to use:** When designing or testing packaging visuals
**How to apply:**
- Lead with clean, minimal design
- Add ONE emotional element (a relevant photo/illustration)
- Include ONE trust/quality indicator
- Don't try to combine everything from every version

## 5. Text & Callout Patterns

### Pattern: Column Layout > Horizontal Layout
**Source:** Internal A/B poll
**Result:** 29 votes for column vs 5 for horizontal layout
**When to use:** When adding benefit callouts or feature text to secondary images
**How to apply:**
- Stack benefits vertically (one per line)
- Use icons/bullets to the left of each benefit
- Keep text short (2-4 words per benefit line)
- Ensure readability at thumbnail size

### Pattern: "30-Day Supply" > "30 Servings"
**Source:** Eugene's feedback on Juana's supplement image
**Result:** Recommended replacing "30 servings" with "30-day supply" for stronger perceived value
**When to use:** Any consumable product (supplements, vitamins, skincare, cleaning supplies)
**How to apply:**
- Frame quantity in terms of duration, not units
- "30-day supply" > "30 servings"
- "3-month supply" > "90 capsules"
- Position this prominently with a background highlight

### Pattern: Communicate Flavor/Variant Visually
**Source:** Hakim's feedback on Dogukan's product
**Result:** "Instead of popping the flavor in orange text, do the powder and a piece of peach to communicate flavor"
**When to use:** Products with flavors, scents, or variants
**How to apply:**
- Show the actual ingredient/flavor visually (real fruit, herbs, etc.)
- Don't rely on text alone to communicate the variant
- Be careful with additions — some categories get flagged for added elements

## 6. Zoom & Detail Patterns

### Pattern: Zoom-In Circle for Key Features
**Source:** Oleg's planned next test for mattress protector; Glow C foot file test
**Result:** Foot file with zoomed-in detail highlighting product features improved all metrics
**When to use:** Products with texture, material quality, or fine details that matter (fabric weave, blade quality, stitching)
**How to apply:**
- Add a circular zoom-in inset showing the key detail
- Position in the lower-right or upper-right corner
- Make the zoom circle large enough to see clearly at thumbnail size
- Use a thin border on the zoom circle (matches product color for cohesion)

### Pattern: Fill the Frame (Strict Rule 5)
**Source:** Eugene's image feedback — "1 is too far away" (Nina); general Slack consensus
**Result:** Products that fill more of the frame consistently get better feedback
**When to use:** EVERY image. This is Strict Rule 5 — product must fill maximum space always.
**How to apply:**
- Product should be as large as possible — fill ALL uncovered white space
- If there's empty space in the image, the product isn't big enough
- For multi-packs: the GROUP of items should fill the frame, not just the hero item
- If product is small, show it at a slight angle to maximize visual presence

## 7. AI Image Generation Patterns

### Pattern: AI for Premium Product Shots
**Source:** H2O Aurora water bottles, Digital Otoscope, multiple Slack examples
**Result:** AI-generated premium images nearly doubled CVR for water bottles; improved CTR/CVR for otoscope
**When to use:** Any product where current photography looks amateur or flat
**How to apply:**
- Use AI to generate studio-quality product shots
- Focus on photorealism, not artistic/stylized looks
- Prompt for specific lighting (soft studio light, rim lighting)
- Always check for AI artifacts: wrong text on labels, extra fingers on hands, warped geometry

### Pattern: Nana Banana / Content Lite for Quick Mockups
**Source:** Nina's workflow shared in Slack — "this is a quick nano banana mockup, please use content lite for a better option"
**Result:** Team uses Nana Banana for fast concept testing, Content Lite for production-ready images
**When to use:** Early-stage concepting before investing in final imagery
**How to apply:**
- Use AI tools for rapid mockup generation
- Test concepts with the team in #team-ab-testing-lab before investing in final production
- Graduate winning concepts to higher-quality tools/designers

### Pattern: Water Spill / Action Element
**Source:** Hakim's suggestion for Swedish Dishcloth
**Result:** "Maybe add a water spill on the second one and make part of the top cloth soak it in partly"
**When to use:** Products where showing the product in action adds clarity (cleaning products, absorbent products, waterproof items)
**How to apply:**
- Add a small, realistic action element (water droplet, spill, steam, etc.)
- The action element MUST be logically connected to the product's purpose (Strict Rule 6) — water on an absorbent product, steam on a cooking tool, etc.
- Keep it subtle — it should enhance understanding, not distract
- Make sure it looks realistic, not clipart-y

## 8. Category-Specific Patterns

### Supplements & Health Products
- Show the bottle/packaging with a premium 3D render
- Add "X-day supply" prominently
- Include key ingredient visuals (not just text)
- "Collagen" or key ingredient in visible text helps CTR (Dragana's observation)
- Consider adding the product next to the box

### Food & Kitchen Products
- Show the product WITH food/ingredients (not empty)
- For multi-packs: stack neatly with slight offset, not all identical front-facing
- Keep it appetizing — warm lighting, fresh ingredients

### Pet Products
- Use Golden Retriever or French Bulldog
- Dog should be visible and engaged with the product
- Dog position matters — don't hide the dog behind the product

### Beauty & Skincare
- Premium renders with highlights are essential
- Show the product applicator (dropper, pump) on the natural side
- Origin indicators work well (Italian, Korean, Japanese)
- Decluttered images with focus on the hero product

### Home & Cleaning Products
- Show the product in a clean, aspirational setting
- For "heavy duty" products, visual cues of strength/power help
- Test action shots (product actively cleaning/working)

## Multi-Pack & Color Variety Patterns

### Pattern: Never Hide the Pack Quantity
**Source:** VI PRESTIGE Moving Bags A/B test — April 2026
**Result:** Single-bag hero concepts failed to communicate the 5-pack value. Customers expect to see what they're buying.
**When to use:** Any product sold as a multi-pack (3-pack, 5-pack, 6-pack, 8-pack, etc.)
**How to apply:**
- Always show all items in the pack, or clearly communicate the quantity
- If using a hero shot, the remaining items MUST be visible behind/beside/below
- Never show just one item when the customer is buying multiple

### Pattern: Color Variety as a Competitive Advantage
**Source:** VI PRESTIGE Moving Bags — only listing with 5 different colors in "moving bags heavy duty" category
**Result:** Color variety is a major differentiator when every competitor shows identical-colored items
**When to use:** Any product that comes in multiple colors as a SET (not individual color options)
**How to apply:**
- Show ALL color variants in the image — this IS the differentiator
- Use creative arrangements to avoid looking like a flat grid: fans, cascades, stacks, hero + lineup
- The color variety should create visual interest that draws the eye at thumbnail size
- Test which color goes in the "hero" position (front/largest)

### Pattern: Smart Multi-Item Arrangements
**Source:** Internal learning — flat grids of identical bags are the category default for moving bags, storage containers
**Result:** Every competitor uses the same grid layout. Any creative arrangement instantly differentiates.
**Arrangement options ranked by visual impact:**
1. **Color Fan/Cascade** — Items fanned out at angles, hero in front. Most dynamic, most premium look.
2. **Hero + Color Lineup** — One large hero, others in a neat row below/beside. Clean and informative.
3. **Stacked Tower** — Items stacked vertically, each color visible. Compact, communicates quantity clearly.
4. **V-Formation** — Items arranged in a V shape with hero at the center front. Dramatic, fills frame well.
5. **Flat Grid** — Standard grid layout. Safest but least differentiated. Only use if others are all doing something different.

## 9. What NOT to Do

### Don't: Use Bad Shadows
Visible shadow edges make everything look cheap. Either do it perfectly or skip it.

### Don't: Overcrowd the Image
More elements =/= better. Every element should earn its place.

### Don't: Make Text Unreadable at Thumbnail Size
If a customer can't read it while scrolling search results on mobile, it's wasted.

### Don't: Rely on the Box Alone
Oleg proved this through 4 tests — the box by itself doesn't convert.

### Don't: Ignore the Competition
Macray's test showed that the competitor's image was simply better — they looked more premium. Always check what the top-ranking competitors look like and aim to stand out or match their quality.

### Don't: Skip Mobile Check
Most Amazon shopping happens on mobile. Always preview your image at mobile thumbnail size before launching a test.

## 10. Polling Insights from Slack

The #team-ab-testing-lab channel uses emoji voting (1/2/3/4 or A/B) to gather quick consensus. Key meta-insights from the voting patterns:

- **Cleaner images win by massive margins** — typically 20:1 or more when there's a clear clutter difference
- **First impressions matter** — Pablo noted that visible vote counts can bias voters (Zoltan suggested hiding them)
- **Thread comments are gold** — the reasoning people give in threads (especially from Nina, Dragana, Oleg, and Hakim) often contains the actual insight, not just the vote
- **"What are you selling?"** — If someone asks this about your image (like Oleg did), that's the strongest signal your image has a clarity problem
- **Multiple perspectives matter** — Different team members catch different things. Dragana catches shadow/quality issues, Nina catches layout/strategy opportunities, Hakim catches communication/messaging gaps
