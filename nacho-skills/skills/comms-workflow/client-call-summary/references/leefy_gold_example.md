## What makes this output good (apply these to future meetings)

1. **Warm opener is meeting-specific.** "recover from the recent setback" reflects a real beat from the meeting. Never copy the example phrasing verbatim — re-anchor on what actually happened today.
2. **Key points are mini-paragraphs, not one-liners.** Each bullet captures: what was discussed → decision → owner → mechanism → why it matters. A bullet that just says "Discussed inventory" is a failure mode.
3. **Owners are tagged inline** with `@Name` (plain text — Slack auto-links if the name matches a workspace user, but we don't resolve user IDs).
4. **Links surface as inline anchors** (`CLICK HERE`, `LINK: HERE`, `HERE`). When the transcript mentions a Drive folder / Calendly / asset link but the URL isn't in the transcript, leave the anchor text followed by a visible `[[FILL: what URL]]` placeholder so Nacho can spot and fill it before sending. Example: `via the creative folder: HERE [[FILL: Leefy creative folder URL]]`. The original Leefy example above predates this convention — going forward use the `[[FILL: …]]` syntax, not bare `HERE`.
5. **"Next steps" lists are crisp action items**, not a re-summary. Each starts with a verb. Sophie's side = what Sophie will execute. Client side = what the client owes. Don't mix.
6. **Closer is warm but short, and varies.** One sentence, matched to the meeting's energy. Forward-looking after a launch, reassuring after a setback, short-and-direct after a routine sync. Never default to "Looking forward to our next call" two recaps in a row.
7. **Tone is executive, not corporate.** First-person from Nacho ("My number 1 structural recommendation"), occasional emphasis ("Important:"), and direct asks to clients ("please share with me ASAP").
